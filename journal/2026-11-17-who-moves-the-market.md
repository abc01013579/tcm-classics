**shuiming:** Who are behind the market motions?

**CC:** Several groups, moving the market in different ways:

- **Steady buyers.** Passive funds and 401k paychecks flow in every two weeks whatever the price: DCA at national scale. Companies buying back their own shares (about $940B for the S&P 500 in 2024) are one of the largest net buyers, pausing in blackout windows around earnings.
- **Institutions with views.** Pensions, mutual funds and sovereign funds are big and slow, rebalancing at month and quarter ends. Hedge funds are leveraged, so when losses hit they cut risk together: the forced selling of 2008 and 2020.
- **Mechanical traders.** Trend followers and volatility-targeting funds sell automatically when volatility rises and buy back when it calms, speeding up crashes like 2020 and August 2024. Options dealers hedge what the public buys; with 0DTE options that hedging can move the index within hours.
- **Liquidity providers.** High-frequency market makers handle roughly half of daily volume. They supply the 1-cent spread but not the direction, and they step back in panics.
- **Retail.** About 20% of volume in recent years, heavy in options; a big factor in TSLA and NVDA, less in SPY.
- **Central banks.** They set the cost of leverage. Fed buying ended the 2020 crash; a Bank of Japan hike set off August 2024.

The long-run trend comes from steady flows: paychecks and buybacks. The crashes come from forced flows: leverage, margin calls and mechanical sellers all needing out at once. The first hour shows which group is in control.
