cc.....That's accurate. Very little in the iPhone was invented by Jobs:

The graphical interface came from Xerox PARC, which Apple saw in 1979.
Multi-touch came from FingerWorks, a small company Apple bought in 2005.
The chips were ARM designs, and the first iPhone's processor was made by Samsung. Today's 3nm chips are made by TSMC.
The networks were built by carriers, and the radio standards came from the telecom industry.
The internet and the web were built by government researchers and Tim Berners-Lee.
His contribution was something else:

Timing. He saw the moment when chips, batteries, touchscreens and mobile networks were just good enough to combine. Earlier attempts like the Apple Newton and Palm failed partly because the parts weren't ready yet.
Judgment about what to leave out. No keyboard, no stylus. Saying no was half the design.
Making the parts work as one. Hardware, software and later the App Store designed together, so the user never sees the seams.
He said it himself, borrowing from Picasso: "Good artists copy, great artists steal."

Over time Apple moved closer to invention. It now designs its own chips (Apple Silicon), though TSMC still manufactures them. The integrator gradually took control of the most important parts.

It's a useful contrast with Thompson. Thompson and Ritchie invented the foundation (Unix, C), and Jobs built on top of foundations like that: macOS and iOS are Unix underneath. Inventors create the parts; integrators see what the parts can become together. Both are rare, and the second rarely gets credit for being a real skill.
