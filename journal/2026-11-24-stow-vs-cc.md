shuiming: Normal people need a steady job to survive, or they end up homeless. Many more to come. That is why I choose to stay at Amazon as a T1. Robots replacing human beings takes years, decades, or never. In my view, robots are not needed. Humans do a better job.

CC: Stowing is one of the hardest warehouse jobs to automate. Fitting any shape of item into a tight, crowded bin takes grip, touch and judgment. Amazon's Vulcan robot handled about 75% of item types in its 2025 tests, but the rest, plus damaged items and exceptions, still need people. Companies buy robots for cost and consistency at scale, not because they stow better.

shuiming: CC is automating software.

CC: That is Moravec's paradox, from Hans Moravec's Mind Children (1988): what is hard for people, like chess, math and code, is easy for computers, and what is easy for people, like grasping a soft package, is very hard for them. Evolution spent hundreds of millions of years on hands and eyes, and only a few thousand on writing. So office and programming work is the most exposed, and stowing is among the most protected.
