**shuiming:** The market is bleeding. Sort of March 2000.

**CC:** March 10, 2000 was the Nasdaq peak, about 5,048, and almost nobody saw it as the top then. It fell about 35% in five weeks, bounced 30–40% that summer, then ground down to about 1,114 by October 2002, roughly 78% below the peak. One −12% day for NVDA confirms nothing. What would: strong bounces that keep failing at lower highs, over months. Many drops felt like the end and weren't, like 2018, 2020 and 2022. March 2000 felt like a dip and was the end.

**shuiming:** It takes guts to handle this kind of market.

**CC:** Guts alone isn't enough. In 2000–02, many brave dip-buyers with too much money got wiped out. What lets people hold on is decided before the drop: position size, no leverage, rules written in calm times, and cash set aside.

**shuiming:** I am lucky that most of my money is in a 401k Vanguard Target 2045, and BRK and AMZN hold up well too. TSM takes a hit, but it won't affect my overall wellbeing.

**CC:** Target 2045 is still about 80% stocks, but it's spread across the whole market. TSM is the most exposed, since NVDA is one of its biggest customers. When a drop doesn't threaten daily life, you aren't forced to sell, so you can just watch.

**shuiming:** I enjoy this kind of drop now. It is a blessing, the first time in my 31 years in the market.

**CC:** For someone who is still buying, a drop is a sale. Feeling it that way, not just knowing it, takes money you don't need to touch and enough years to have seen drops recover.
