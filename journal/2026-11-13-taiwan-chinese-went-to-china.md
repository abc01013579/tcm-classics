shuiming…..many Taiwan Chinese citizens went to China: Kaifu Li , Andrew Yao etc.

cc….. Good point. There's real precedent:

	•	林毅夫 (Justin Lin) swam from Kinmen in 1979 and became chief economist of the World Bank. You probably know his work from 人大.
	•	姚期智 (Andrew Yao) grew up in Taiwan, won the Turing Award, moved to Tsinghua in 2004, and later gave up his U.S. citizenship.
	•	In chips specifically: 张汝京 founded SMIC, and 梁孟松 and 蒋尚义 went from TSMC to SMIC.

So for a Taiwanese engineer, going to the mainland isn't unthinkable. It's a well-worn road.

The difference for Jensen is timing and status. Those people moved before the 2022 U.S.-person chip rules, and none of them had an FBI probe open. For him the road exists, but the toll is much higher.

shuiming….. Foxconn

cc….. Foxconn (郭台铭, Terry Gou) is the biggest Taiwan-to-mainland story of all. He went in during the late 1980s, built Shenzhen Longhua into a factory city, and became the iPhone's assembler, employing about a million mainland workers at the peak. Gou even ran for president in Taiwan later on.

There's an interesting twist: Foxconn is now one of Nvidia's biggest partners, building its AI servers. If Jensen ever wanted a home base with factories, Taiwan roots, and mainland experience, Foxconn's world is the natural door. The difference, again, is that Foxconn builds things. The trouble comes when a business moves advanced chip know-how to China.
