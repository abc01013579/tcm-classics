Here is a summary of the document "[斯坦福的高效睡眠法]":

Overview and Background: Authored by Seiji Nishino (Professor at Stanford University's Department of Psychiatry and Director of the SCN Laboratory), the book introduces scientific sleep research from Stanford over the past 30 years to help readers achieve optimal sleep and improve daytime productivity.

The Concept of "Sleep Debt": The book warns against accumulating "sleep debt" (chronic sleep deprivation). Similar to financial debt, small daily sleep deficits accumulate and can cause serious cognitive and physiological impairments, such as "momentary sleep" (micro-sleeps lasting a few seconds), which can be more dangerous than drunk driving. It notes that short-duration sleep is largely determined by genetics, and forcing oneself to sleep less can harm health, increase mortality risk, and lead to weight gain.

The Five Missions of Sleep: Sleep serves five vital functions:

Giving the brain and body rest by allowing the parasympathetic nervous system to take charge.

Organizing and consolidating memories (transferring information from the hippocampus to the cerebral cortex).

Regulating hormone balance (such as growth hormone, leptin, and ghrelin).

Boosting immunity and helping ward off illnesses.

Clearing out brain waste products (such as beta-amyloid proteins linked to Alzheimer's disease via the cerebrospinal fluid).

The "Golden 90 Minutes" Rule: The most critical determinant of sleep quality is not the 90-minute REM/non-REM cycle length, but rather the first 90 minutes of deep non-REM sleep after falling asleep. Ensuring high quality during this initial deep sleep period regulates autonomic nerves, triggers peak growth hormone secretion, and dictates the overall quality of the remaining sleep cycle.

Sleep Switches (Body Temperature and Brain): To achieve optimal sleep and successfully enter the golden 90 minutes, two main "switches" must be controlled:

Body Temperature Switch: Deep sleep is facilitated when the difference between core body temperature and skin temperature shrinks (by warming the hands and feet to release internal heat).

Brain Switch: Calming the brain and shutting down mental excitation caused by stress, digital devices, or late-night cognitive activity helps ensure a smooth transition into deep sleep.
